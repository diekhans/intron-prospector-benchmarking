#+TITLE: Why WTC11 SJ.out.tab junctions are missing from intron-prospector calls
#+DATE: 2026-11-17

* Conclusion

STAR copies the Illumina chastity filter flag into SAM flag 0x200 and counts
those alignments in SJ.out.tab.  intron-prospector skips them unless
--include=qcfail names them.  All 20 junctions in a random sample are supported
only by alignments the default run skips: 17 by 0x200 alignments alone, 3 by
0x200 plus secondary alignments.  None is a case of intron-prospector reading an
alignment and failing to report its junction.

* Sample

- Population: the 3,060 WTC11 junctions in SJ.out.tab that the base calls file
  does not contain at any threshold, from output/WTC11.base.raw.compare.tsv.gz
  with found = star, restricted to star_uniq at least 1 and star_overhang at
  least 12.
- 20 drawn with random.Random(20261115).sample.
- All 20 have star_uniq 1, star_overhang runs 12 to 50, 19 are GT/AG and 1 is
  GC/AG, 19 are annotated and 1 is novel.

* Method

- Indexed the BAM into data/WTC11.illumina.bam.bai, since its own directory is
  not writable.
- For each junction, fetched every alignment covering the last exon base, parsed
  all N operations out of the CIGAR, and kept those whose N matches the junction
  exactly.
- Classified each supporting alignment by the flags intron-prospector acts on.

* Flags

The difference is which SAM FLAG bits each tool acts on.  Only four bits matter
here, and only two of them are set in this BAM.  The intron-prospector column
is what process_alignment does with the bit; --include names a category to read
and --exclude one to skip.

|   bit | name          | set in this BAM                                                          | SJ.out.tab                | intron-prospector                                               |
|-------+---------------+--------------------------------------------------------------------------+---------------------------+-----------------------------------------------------------------|
| 0x200 | QCFAIL        | yes, by STAR from the Illumina chastity filter field in the FASTQ header | counted in uniq and multi | skipped, unless --include=qcfail                                |
| 0x100 | SECONDARY     | yes, for the other loci of a multi-mapping read                          | counted in multi          | skipped, unless --include=secondary; counted as multi mapped    |
| 0x400 | DUP           | no                                                                       | n/a                       | skipped, unless --include=dup                                   |
| 0x800 | SUPPLEMENTARY | no                                                                       | n/a                       | read, counted as multi mapped; --exclude=supplementary skips it |

0x1, 0x2 and 0x8 are read only to decide a read category: a proper pair is
categorised by its NH tag, anything else is counted as unsure.

Counts by flag:

| class                       | supporting alignments, 20 sampled junctions | alignments in chr1:100912000-100914000 |
|-----------------------------+---------------------------------------------+----------------------------------------|
| QCFAIL 0x200                |                                          29 | 34 of 559, 6.1%                        |
| SECONDARY 0x100             |                                          23 | 89 of 559, 15.9%                       |
| both bits                   |                                           6 | 2                                      |
| DUP 0x400                   |                                           0 | 0                                      |
| SUPPLEMENTARY 0x800         |                                           0 | 0                                      |
| none of these, read by both |                                           0 | 438                                    |

Anchor length separates the two set bits:

- The 23 alignments with 0x200 alone have anchors 1 to 50, median 32, matching
  the star_overhang values 12 to 50 in the sample.  They are ordinary
  alignments that differ only in the filter bit.
- The 23 with 0x100, 6 of which also have 0x200, have anchors 1 to 3, median 1.
  An anchor threshold removes them whether or not --include=secondary reads
  them.

* The chastity filter

Chastity is a per base call measure of signal purity on the sequencer, computed
from the cluster image, not from the alignment.

- Chastity of one base call is the brightest channel intensity divided by the
  sum of the brightest and second brightest.  It runs from 0.5, two channels
  equally bright, to 1.0, one channel only.
- Illumina's purity filter marks a cluster as not passing when more than one of
  the first 25 cycles has chastity below 0.6.  The usual cause is two
  overlapping clusters, so the base calls come from a mixture of templates.
- The read is still base called and still written to the FASTQ.  The filter
  result is the second field of the header comment, 1:Y:0:INDEX, where Y means
  the read is filtered, that is, it did not pass.  N means it passed.
- bcl2fastq and bcl-convert write all reads by default, so a FASTQ holds both
  classes unless the run was demultiplexed with a pass-filter-only option.
- Chastity says nothing about mapping.  A chastity filtered read can align
  uniquely with a full length match, as the 0x200 alignments above do.

* Why this matters

- 0x200 alone accounts for the WTC11 difference.  Every sampled junction has at
  least one QCFAIL alignment and no alignment with none of these bits set.
  Clearing 0x200 on one alignment makes its junction appear.
- Both tools count a read once per junction, so a count difference is a read
  set difference: the default run counts neither the QC fail nor the secondary
  alignments SJ.out.tab counts.  With --include=qcfail,secondary the read sets
  match and the counts are equal on 99.1% of the junctions the two share.
- SECONDARY is a cause of its own at other junctions, see
  doc/wtc11-flair-only.org, but not of these.  In this sample no junction is
  supported by secondary alignments alone, and their anchors of 1 to 3 are
  below any threshold worth comparing.
- Which read set to compare is a choice, not a bug fix in either tool.
  --include=qcfail reads the QC fail alignments; the alternative is filtering
  the BAM for both tools.
- The effect scales with the fraction of reads the sequencer flagged, 5.3% in
  this FASTQ.  A dataset with no chastity filter flags will not show it.

* Root cause

1. The FASTQ records the Illumina filter flag: 5,336 of 100,000 sampled headers
   in WTC11-illumina-all_1.fastq read 1:Y:0:..., the did-not-pass form.
   The one alignment supporting chr1:100912246-100913662 is read
   HISEQ:1286:HKCGCBCX3:2:1109:15948:36588, whose header in
   WTC11-illumina-all_2.fastq is 2:Y:0:CGAGGCTGGCGTAAGA.
2. STAR sets SAM flag 0x200 from that flag.
3. STAR still aligns those reads and still counts them in SJ.out.tab.  The
   junction above has star_uniq 1 from exactly this one alignment.
4. intron-prospector skips them.  In the intron-prospector tree,
   src/junctions_extractor.cc, process_alignment ignores any alignment whose
   flags meet exclude_flags_, which --include and --exclude set from
   ALIGN_CATS_EXCLUDED_DEFAULT, BAM_FQCFAIL, BAM_FDUP and BAM_FSECONDARY.
   junction_qc tests only the intron and anchor lengths.

* Evidence

- Single alignment reproduction.  One SAM record, flag 675, which is 0x200 plus
  proper pair, second in pair and mate reverse:

  : samtools view -X -b -o one.bam BAM BAI chr1:100912000-100914000
  : intron-prospector --min-confidence-score=0.0 --sj-filter=all \
  :     --min-anchor-length=1 --min-intron-length=20 --max-intron-length=1000000 \
  :     --intron-calls=one.calls.tsv one.bam

  The calls file has a header and no rows.  Clearing 0x200 from that one flag,
  675 to 163, and nothing else, makes intron-prospector report the junction with
  uniq_mapped_count 1 and overhangs 32 and 68.  That is the minimum controlling
  change.

- Region reproduction.  chr1:100912000-100914000 holds 559 alignments with 10
  distinct N operations.  Counting each N operation by the class of the
  alignment it comes from, where usable means neither 0x200 nor secondary:

  | intron_start | intron_end | usable | 0x200 | secondary | reported |

- Rate.  34 of the 559 alignments in that region have 0x200 set, 6.1%, against
  5.3% of reads flagged in the FASTQ.

- Per read link.  The read behind the 0x200 alignment in the single alignment
  test is flagged Y in the FASTQ, so the chain from FASTQ to flag to skipped
  alignment is closed on one junction, not only by rate.

* Scope

- 0 of 20 sampled junctions point at a defect in junction extraction itself.
- The sample covers junctions absent from the base calls at any threshold:
  4,209 of the 6,376 that SJ.out.tab holds and the calls do not under
  filt_similar.  The other 2,167 are in the calls and are removed by the anchor
  and unique-read thresholds.
- Every dataset whose FASTQ records Illumina filter flags will show this.  A549
  has none: no alignment of the 8,529,272 sampled from three 20 Mb regions
  has 0x200, and its base and qcfail calls files are byte identical.

* Consequences

- The default run and SJ.out.tab count different read sets.  SJ.out.tab counts
  chastity filtered reads; the default run skips them.  This accounts for the
  bulk of the WTC11 junctions STAR reports and the default run does not.
- --include=qcfail reads those alignments.  The alternative, filtering the BAM
  before both tools, needs another pass over it and another copy.
- Secondary alignments account for nearly all of the rest, see
  doc/wtc11-flair-only.org.

* What --include does to the comparison

Each dataset gets one pass per run, base, qcfail and secondary, each compared
against the same SJ.out.tab; the numbers are from
output/WTC11.<run>.<filter>.summary.tsv, main chromosomes only.

| run       | filter       |    STAR | intron-prospector | in STAR, not in the calls | in the calls, not in STAR |
|-----------+--------------+---------+-------------------+---------------------------+---------------------------|
| base      | raw          | 383,247 |           365,603 |                    17,648 |                         4 |
| qcfail    | raw          | 383,247 |           370,242 |                    13,009 |                         4 |
| secondary | raw          | 383,247 |           383,256 |                         3 |                        12 |
| base      | filt_similar | 356,424 |           350,080 |                     6,376 |                        32 |
| qcfail    | filt_similar | 356,424 |           356,191 |                       270 |                        37 |
| secondary | filt_similar | 356,424 |           356,462 |                         6 |                        44 |

- Under raw, --include=qcfail takes the junctions SJ.out.tab holds and the
  calls do not from 17,648 to 13,009, and --include=qcfail,secondary takes it
  to 3.  The two read sets are then the same one and the two junction sets
  agree to within 15 junctions out of 383,247.
- Under filt_similar, which applies the same anchor and unique read thresholds
  to both sides, the same gap is 6,376, 270 and 6.  A secondary alignment is
  counted as multi mapping, so reading it moves few junctions over the unique
  read threshold; most of the drop from 6,376 is the QC fail alignments.
- The calls file grows with the read set: 366,631 junctions on base, 371,285 on
  qcfail and 384,808 on secondary, over all targets rather than the main
  chromosomes the table counts.
- Both sides count a read once per junction, so the counts are comparable under
  secondary: equal on 379,690 of the 383,244 shared junctions, 99.1%, and never
  higher in the calls.  The remaining 3,554 are alignments whose anchor holds
  an insertion, deletion or clip, which STAR counts and intron-prospector
  rejects.
