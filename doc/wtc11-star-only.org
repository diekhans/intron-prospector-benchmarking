#+TITLE: Why WTC11 SJ.out.tab junctions are missing from intron-prospector calls
#+DATE: 2026-11-15

* Conclusion

STAR copies the Illumina chastity filter flag into SAM flag 0x200, counts those
alignments in SJ.out.tab, and intron-prospector skips them.  All 20 junctions in
a random sample were supported only by alignments intron-prospector skips: 15 by
0x200 alignments alone, 5 by 0x200 plus secondary alignments.  None was a case
of intron-prospector reading an alignment and failing to report its junction.

* Sample

- Population: the 4,211 WTC11 junctions in SJ.out.tab that the calls file does
  not contain at any threshold, from output/WTC11.raw.compare.tsv.gz with
  found = star, restricted to star_uniq at least 1 and star_overhang at least 12.
- 20 drawn with random.Random(20261115).sample.
- All 20 are annotated, 18 have star_uniq 1 and 2 have star_uniq 2, star_overhang
  runs 15 to 49, 19 are GT/AG and 1 is GC/AG.

* Method

- Indexed the BAM into benchmark/data/WTC11.illumina.bam.bai, since its own
  directory is not writable.
- For each junction, fetched every alignment covering the last exon base, parsed
  all N operations out of the CIGAR, and kept those whose N matches the junction
  exactly.
- Classified each supporting alignment by the flags intron-prospector acts on.

| supported only by QC fail and secondary alignments |  5 |

* Flags

The difference is which SAM FLAG bits each tool acts on.  Only four bits matter
here, and only two of them are set in this BAM.

|   bit | name          | set in this BAM                                                          | SJ.out.tab                | intron-prospector                                                |
|-------+---------------+--------------------------------------------------------------------------+---------------------------+------------------------------------------------------------------|
| 0x200 | QCFAIL        | yes, by STAR from the Illumina chastity filter field in the FASTQ header | counted in uniq and multi | alignment never read, process_alignment                          |
| 0x100 | SECONDARY     | yes, for the other loci of a multi-mapping read                          | counted in multi          | junction not recorded, junction_qc; read counted as multi mapped |
| 0x400 | DUP           | no                                                                       | n/a                       | alignment never read, process_alignment                          |
| 0x800 | SUPPLEMENTARY | no                                                                       | n/a                       | read counted as multi mapped                                     |

0x1, 0x2 and 0x8 are read only to decide a read category: a proper pair is
categorised by its NH tag, anything else is counted as unsure.

Counts by flag:

| class                       | supporting alignments, 20 sampled junctions | alignments in chr1:100912000-100914000 |
|-----------------------------+---------------------------------------------+----------------------------------------|
| QCFAIL 0x200                |                                          27 | 34 of 559, 6.1%                        |
| SECONDARY 0x100             |                                          18 | 89 of 559, 15.9%                       |
| both bits                   |                                           0 | 2                                      |
| DUP 0x400                   |                                           0 | 0                                      |
| SUPPLEMENTARY 0x800         |                                           0 | 0                                      |
| none of these, read by both |                                           0 | 438                                    |

Anchor length separates the two set bits:

- The 27 QCFAIL alignments have anchors 3 to 49, median 32, matching the
  star_overhang values 15 to 49 in the sample.  They are ordinary alignments
  that differ only in the filter bit.
- All 18 SECONDARY alignments have an anchor of 1 or 2.  They would be removed
  by any anchor threshold even if intron-prospector read them.

* The chastity filter

Chastity is a per base call measure of signal purity on the sequencer, computed
from the cluster image, not from the alignment.

- Chastity of one base call is the brightest channel intensity divided by the
  sum of the brightest and second brightest.  It runs from 0.5, two channels
  equally bright, to 1.0, one channel only.
- Illumina's purity filter marks a cluster as not passing when more than one of
  the first 25 cycles has chastity below 0.6.  The usual cause is two
  overlapping clusters, so the base calls come from a mixture of templates.
- The read is still base called and still written to the FASTQ.  The filter
  result is the second field of the header comment, 1:Y:0:INDEX, where Y means
  the read is filtered, that is, it did not pass.  N means it passed.
- bcl2fastq and bcl-convert write all reads by default, so a FASTQ holds both
  classes unless the run was demultiplexed with a pass-filter-only option.
- Chastity says nothing about mapping.  A chastity filtered read can align
  uniquely with a full length match, as the 27 alignments above do.

* Why this matters

- 0x200 alone accounts for the WTC11 difference.  Every sampled junction has at
  least one QCFAIL alignment and no alignment with none of these bits set.
  Clearing 0x200 on one alignment makes its junction appear.
- The two tools report counts over different read sets, so the junction counts
  are not directly comparable, nor are read-count thresholds applied to them.
- SECONDARY is not a second cause.  No sampled junction is supported by
  secondary alignments alone, and their anchors of 1 or 2 are below any
  threshold worth comparing.
- The fix is a choice of read set, not a bug fix in either tool.  Either filter
  the BAM for both tools, or add an intron-prospector option to include QCFAIL
  alignments.
- The effect scales with the fraction of reads the sequencer flagged, 5.3% in
  this FASTQ.  A dataset with no chastity filter flags will not show it.

* Root cause

1. The FASTQ records the Illumina filter flag: 5,336 of 100,000 sampled headers
   in WTC11-illumina-all_1.fastq read 1:Y:0:..., the did-not-pass form.
   The one alignment supporting chr1:100912246-100913662 is read
   HISEQ:1286:HKCGCBCX3:2:1109:15948:36588, whose header in
   WTC11-illumina-all_2.fastq is 2:Y:0:CGAGGCTGGCGTAAGA.
2. STAR sets SAM flag 0x200 from that flag.
3. STAR still aligns those reads and still counts them in SJ.out.tab.  The
   junction above has star_uniq 1 from exactly this one alignment.
4. intron-prospector skips them.  In the intronProspector tree,
   src/junctions_extractor.cc:

   - process_alignment ignores any alignment with BAM_FQCFAIL or BAM_FDUP
   - get_read_category signals a logic error if one reaches it
   - junction_qc rejects BAM_FSECONDARY

* Evidence

- Single alignment reproduction.  One SAM record, flag 675, which is 0x200 plus
  proper pair, second in pair and mate reverse:

  : samtools view -X -b -o one.bam BAM BAI chr1:100912000-100914000
  : intron-prospector --min-confidence-score=0.0 --sj-filter=all \
  :     --min-anchor-length=1 --min-intron-length=20 --max-intron-length=1000000 \
  :     --intron-calls=one.calls.tsv one.bam

  The calls file has a header and no rows.  Clearing 0x200 from that one flag,
  675 to 163, and nothing else, makes intron-prospector report the junction with
  uniq_mapped_count 1 and overhangs 32 and 68.  That is the minimum controlling
  change.

- Region reproduction.  chr1:100912000-100914000 holds 559 alignments with 10
  distinct N operations.  Counting each N operation by the class of the
  alignment it comes from, where usable means neither 0x200 nor secondary:

  | intron_start | intron_end | usable | 0x200 | secondary | reported |

- Rate.  34 of the 559 alignments in that region have 0x200 set, 6.1%, against
  5.3% of reads flagged in the FASTQ.

- Per read link.  The read behind the 0x200 alignment in the single alignment
  test is flagged Y in the FASTQ, so the chain from FASTQ to flag to skipped
  alignment is closed on one junction, not only by rate.

* Scope

- 0 of 20 sampled junctions pointed at a defect in junction extraction itself.
- The sample covers the 4,211 junctions absent from the calls, not the other
  2,168 STAR-only junctions under filt_similar, which are in the calls and are
  removed by the anchor and unique-read thresholds.
- Every dataset whose FASTQ records Illumina filter flags will show this.  A549
  has none: no alignment of the 8,529,272 sampled from three 20 Mb regions
  has 0x200, and its base and qcfail calls files are byte identical.

* Consequences

- The two tools count different read sets.  STAR's SJ.out.tab includes chastity
  filtered reads; intron-prospector excludes them.  This accounts for the bulk of
  the WTC11 junctions STAR reports and intron-prospector does not.
- intron-prospector takes --include=qcfail, which reads those alignments
  rather than skipping them.  The alternative, filtering the BAM before both
  tools, needs another pass over it and another copy.

* What --include=qcfail does to the comparison

The benchmark runs each dataset twice, base and qcfail, and compares each
against the same SJ.out.tab; the numbers are from
output/WTC11.<run>.<filter>.summary.tsv, main chromosomes only.

| run    | filter       |    STAR | intron-prospector | in STAR, not in the calls |
|--------+--------------+---------+-------------------+---------------------------|
| base   | raw          | 383,247 |           365,603 |                    17,648 |
| qcfail | raw          | 383,247 |           370,242 |                    13,009 |
| base   | filt_similar | 356,424 |           350,080 |                     6,376 |
| qcfail | filt_similar | 356,424 |           356,191 |                       270 |

- Under filt_similar, which applies the same anchor and unique read thresholds
  to both sides, the junctions STAR reports and the calls do not fall from
  6,376 to 270, and the two sets are then within 233 junctions of each other
  out of 356,424.
- Under raw the gap stays at 13,009, since SJ.out.tab also holds annotated
  junctions that bypass STAR's own output filters and junctions whose only
  support is below the thresholds the calls keep.  Those are a different
  cause, not this one.
- The calls file grows by 4,654 junctions, 366,631 to 371,285, and the
  junctions intron-prospector reports that STAR does not stay at 4 under raw.
